\paragraph{Continual Harness as-is makes the small model worse.}
With a fixed harness, qwen passes 32\% of the tasks. When it edits its own harness (C1), it passes
only 15\%. Pair by pair, C1 is worse than C0 on 15 (task, seed) pairs and better on only 5, which is
significant ($p = 0.041$). The drop is similar on train tasks (33\% to 16\%) and held-out tasks
(27\% to 13\%), so the edits do not even help on the tasks they were learned from.

\paragraph{No single technique is enough.}
Every technique alone leaves qwen below the fixed harness. Four of them reduce the harm of C1: a
separate refiner (22\%), the regression gate (20\%), the memory database (20\%) and verified skills
(17\%). None of these is significantly different from C1 (all $p > 0.4$; Table~\ref{tab:vsc1}).
Structured errors alone makes things worse (8\%, significantly below C0).

\paragraph{All five together recover the fixed-harness level.}
With all five techniques (C8), qwen passes 35\% of the tasks, level with the fixed harness (14 pairs
better, 12 worse, $p = 0.845$). Compared with Continual Harness as-is, C8 is \emph{significantly
better}: 18 pairs better and 6 worse ($p = 0.023$; Table~\ref{tab:vsc1}). It is the only condition
that is significantly better than C1.

\paragraph{The full system does best on held-out tasks, but the sample is small.}
On the 5 held-out tasks, C8 passes 9 of 15 runs (60\%), against 4 of 15 (27\%) for the fixed
harness. Pair by pair this is 7 better and 2 worse, which is \emph{not} significant ($p = 0.18$): the
confidence intervals overlap (36--80\% and 11--52\%). We treat it as a promising sign, not a result.
On train tasks, C8 is slightly below C0 (27\% against 33\%).

\paragraph{Reflexion-style memory is the worst condition.}
When qwen writes reflections about its own failures and reads them on later tasks, it passes only
5\% of the tasks, and none of the held-out runs. It is worse than C0 on 17 pairs and better on 1
($p < 0.001$). We look at why in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{The main finding repeats on a second model.}
Gemma is much stronger: with the fixed harness it already passes 92\% of the tasks. Continual
Harness as-is again makes it significantly worse, dropping to 77\% (11 pairs worse, 2 better,
$p = 0.022$). So self-editing harms both a weak and a strong small model.

\paragraph{The full system helps less on the strong model.}
C8 reaches 80\% on gemma, slightly above C1 (11 pairs better, 9 worse, $p = 0.82$) and still below
the fixed harness (not significant, $p = 0.065$). On held-out tasks C8 gives 73\%, between C1
(60\%) and C0 (80\%). With a baseline this close to 100\%, there is little room for any gain.

\subsection{What Happens to the Edits}

Table~\ref{tab:edits} shows how often the refiner ran, what the regression gate decided, and what
the final harness looked like.

\begin{table}[t]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{llccl}
\toprule
Model & Condition & Refiner runs & Gate: kept / undone & Final harness (per seed) \\
\midrule
qwen  & C1              & 17 & -- & strategy 2.8k--3.3k chars, 1--4 skills, 1--5 sub-agents \\
qwen  & + regression gate & 16 & 13 / 3 & one seed ends with the starting strategy \\
qwen  & C8              & 14 & 11 / 3 & strategy 3.5k--4.0k chars, 0--3 skills, 3--5 sub-agents \\
gemma & C1              & 6  & -- & strategy 2.4k--3.5k chars, 1--5 skills, 1--3 sub-agents \\
gemma & C8              & 6  & 1 / 5  & \textbf{2 of 3 seeds: exactly the starting harness} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Edits over all 3 seeds. ``Refiner runs'' counts the times the four passes ran. The gate
compares the old and new harness on up to 3 recent train tasks.}
\label{tab:edits}
\end{table}

\paragraph{For qwen, the gate cannot tell good edits from bad ones.}
In the regression-gate run, the gate kept 13 of 16 edits, but 10 of the 13 were \emph{ties}: 7 times
both harnesses passed 0 of the rerun tasks, and 3 times both passed 1. Only 3 kept edits were real
gains. In C8, the gate kept 11 of 14, and again 9 of the 11 were ties. When the model fails most
tasks, a test on 3 recent tasks gives almost no signal, and most edits pass the test by default.

\paragraph{For gemma, the gate rejects almost everything.}
In C8 on gemma, the gate undid 5 of 6 edits, because each time the new harness passed fewer of the
rerun tasks than the old one. The one edit it kept was a tie. In 2 of the 3 seeds, the final harness
was exactly the starting harness. This explains why C8 on gemma behaves much like the fixed harness:
for those seeds, the only differences from C0 are the structured error messages and the sampling
noise.

\paragraph{Gemma learns from fewer steps.}
The refiner ran only 6 times on gemma against 14--18 times on qwen, because gemma solves tasks in
about 5 steps (qwen uses 10--14), and edits are scheduled every 25 steps.

\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Forgetting: train tasks passed during learning that failed at evaluation.}
\label{tab:forgetting}

\paragraph{Forgetting is high for qwen in every condition.}
More than half of the tasks qwen solved while learning failed again at evaluation, in every
condition, with or without the gate. For a weak model, part of this is plain chance: a model that
passes a task one time in three will often fail it the next time, even with the same harness. So
forgetting alone cannot separate the effect of the edits from this noise. Still, C8 passed the
most train tasks while learning (18 of 45, against 12 for C1).

\paragraph{For gemma, the gate halves forgetting.}
Gemma passes almost every train task while learning. With C1, 7 of those 42 then fail at
evaluation; with C8, only 3 of 40 do. Because the gate undid most edits, fewer changes could break
tasks that already worked.

Table~\ref{tab:cost} shows behaviour and cost.

\paragraph{Failing conditions get stuck.}
With the fixed harness, qwen hits the 15-step limit in 29 of 60 runs. With C1 it hits it in 53 of 60:
instead of finishing, it keeps calling tools until it runs out of steps. The conditions with the
lowest success (structured errors, Reflexion) hit the limit most often (51 and 54 of 60). C8 is the
only learning condition that stays close to the fixed harness (31 of 60), and it is also the only
one that matches its success rate.

\paragraph{Self-editing makes the prompt much longer.}
With C1, qwen uses three times as many prompt tokens per task as with the fixed harness (44{,}875
against 14{,}240), because of the longer strategy and the extra memory, skills and sub-agents, and
because more steps are used. Gemma's prompts grow 2.5 times (3{,}403 to 8{,}400). In C8 the growth
is smaller on both models.

\paragraph{The full system costs about twice the time on qwen.}
C8 takes 28.1 seconds per task against 14.5 for the fixed harness, plus about 24 minutes of learning
per seed (refiner calls and gate runs). On gemma C8 is even slightly faster than C1, because most
edits were undone.

\paragraph{Format errors are rare, except after edits.}
Calls to a tool that does not exist were almost absent for qwen (1 at evaluation in all runs) and
for gemma with the fixed harness and C8 (0). Gemma with C1 made 14 such calls (see below).

\paragraph{The small model rarely uses what the refiner builds.}
In C1, qwen's own refiner wrote skills, but qwen called them only 3 times in total. Skills were used
more when they were written by the larger refiner (45 calls) or when memory was retrieved by
relevance (20 calls). With verified skills, the check itself worked: it rejected 8 broken skills,
for example code that used folder paths that do not exist. But qwen still called the kept skills only
3 times. With structured errors alone, qwen never called a skill or a sub-agent.

\paragraph{Gemma sometimes calls skills by the wrong name.}
Gemma with C1 made 14 calls to unknown tools at evaluation. In these calls it used the \emph{name of
a skill or sub-agent} (for example \texttt{file\_system\_organizer}) as if it were a tool, instead of
calling it through \texttt{run\_skill} or \texttt{call\_subagent}. The edited harness thus added a
new kind of error that the fixed harness never had.
