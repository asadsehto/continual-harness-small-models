\paragraph{On held-out tasks, only C8 is above the fixed harness, and the sample is small.}
On the 5 held-out tasks, C8 passes 9 of 15 runs (60\%), against 4 of 15 (27\%) for the fixed
harness. Pair by pair this is 7 better and 2 worse, which is \emph{not} significant ($p = 0.18$),
and the confidence intervals overlap (36--80\% and 11--52\%). C8 is above C0 on held-out tasks in
each of the 3 seeds (Table~\ref{tab:seeds}), which is why we find the result interesting, but it
is suggestive and needs more held-out tasks to confirm. All other conditions are at or below the
fixed harness on held-out tasks.

\paragraph{Continual Harness as-is lowers success.}
With a fixed harness, qwen passes 32\% of all tasks. When it edits its own harness (C1), it passes
15\%. C1 is lower than C0 in every seed (3, 2 and 4 passes against 8, 4 and 7;
Table~\ref{tab:seeds}). Pair by pair, C1 is worse on 15 (task, seed) pairs and better on 5
($p = 0.041$). This is a nominal result: after Holm correction for the 8 comparisons with C0, the
adjusted $p$ is 0.25. The drop is similar on train tasks (33\% to 16\%) and held-out tasks (27\% to
13\%), so the edits did not even help on the tasks they were learned from.

\paragraph{No single technique is enough.}
Every technique alone leaves qwen below the fixed harness on all tasks. Four of them have higher
success than C1: a separate refiner (22\%), the regression gate (20\%), the memory database
(20\%) and verified skills (17\%). None of these differs significantly from C1 (all unadjusted
$p > 0.4$; Table~\ref{tab:vsc1}). Structured errors alone gives the lowest success of the single
techniques (8\%), significantly below C0 after correction.

\paragraph{All five together are level with the fixed harness and above C1.}
With all five techniques (C8), qwen passes 35\% of all tasks. This is not different from the fixed
harness (14 pairs better, 12 worse, $p = 0.85$). Note that ``not different'' is not the same as
``equal'': with 60 runs we cannot rule out a difference of several points either way. Compared with
Continual Harness as-is, C8 has higher success in every seed (7, 6 and 8 passes against 3, 2 and 4)
and is better on 18 pairs and worse on 6 ($p = 0.023$; Table~\ref{tab:vsc1}). This is the only
comparison with C1 that is nominally significant; after Holm correction for the 6 comparisons with
C1 it is not ($p = 0.14$). On train tasks, C8 is slightly below C0 (27\% against 33\%).

\paragraph{Reflexion-style memory is the worst condition.}
When qwen writes reflections about its own failures and reads them on later tasks, it passes only
5\% of the tasks, and none of the held-out runs. It is worse than C0 on 17 pairs and better on 1
(adjusted $p = 0.001$). We discuss possible reasons in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\paragraph{The main finding repeats on a second model.}
Gemma is much stronger: with the fixed harness it already passes 92\% of the tasks. Continual
Harness as-is again lowers success, to 77\% (11 pairs worse, 2 better, $p = 0.022$; adjusted
$p = 0.045$). It is lower in 2 seeds and equal in the third (16, 14 and 16 passes against 19, 20
and 16). So, on our tasks, both a weak and a strong small model are below the floor as we define it
in Section~\ref{sec:intro}.

\paragraph{The full system helps less on the strong model.}
C8 reaches 80\% on gemma, slightly above C1 (11 pairs better, 9 worse, $p = 0.82$) and still below
the fixed harness (2 pairs better, 9 worse, $p = 0.065$). On held-out tasks C8 gives 73\%, between
C1 (60\%) and C0 (80\%). With a baseline this close to 100\%, there is little room for any gain.

\paragraph{No held-out difference is significant.}
On held-out tasks, the largest difference is C8 against C1 on qwen (9 pairs better, 2 worse,

\paragraph{Seeds vary a lot for some conditions.}
Most conditions give similar results in each seed, but some do not. The memory database on qwen

\label{tab:edits}

\paragraph{For qwen, the gate gives little signal.}
In the regression-gate run, the gate kept 13 of 16 edits, but 10 of the 13 were \emph{ties}: 7
times both harnesses passed 0 of the rerun tasks, and 3 times both passed 1. Only 3 kept edits
improved the rerun score. In C8, the gate kept 11 of 14, and again 9 of the 11 were ties. When the
model fails most tasks, a test on 3 recent tasks gives almost no signal, and because a tie counts as
a pass, most edits are kept by default.

\paragraph{For gemma, the gate rejects almost everything.}
In C8 on gemma, the gate undid 5 of 6 edits, because each time the new harness passed fewer of the
rerun tasks than the old one. The one edit it kept was a tie. In 2 of the 3 seeds, the final harness
was exactly the starting harness. For those seeds, the only differences between C8 and C0 at
evaluation are the structured error messages and run-to-run drift. This fits with C8 on gemma
behaving much like the fixed harness.

\paragraph{Gemma learns from fewer steps.}
The refiner ran only 6 times on gemma against 14--18 times on qwen, because gemma solves tasks in
about 5 steps (qwen uses 10--14), and edits are scheduled every 25 steps.

\paragraph{Forgetting is high for qwen in every condition.}
More than half of the tasks qwen solved while learning failed again at evaluation, in every
condition, with or without the gate. For a weak model, much of this can be chance: a model that
passes a task one time in three will often fail it the next time, even with the same harness. So
forgetting alone cannot separate the effect of the edits from this noise. C8 passed the most train
tasks while learning (18 of 45, against 12 for C1).

\paragraph{For gemma, forgetting is lower with C8.}
Gemma passes almost every train task while learning. With C1, 7 of those 42 then fail at
evaluation; with C8, 3 of 40 do. This fits with the gate undoing most edits, so that fewer changes
could break tasks that already worked, but counts this small could also differ by chance.

Table~\ref{tab:cost} shows behaviour and cost.

\paragraph{Failing conditions get stuck.}
With the fixed harness, qwen hits the 15-step limit in 29 of 60 runs. With C1 it hits it in 53 of
60: instead of finishing, it keeps calling tools until it runs out of steps. The conditions with the
lowest success (structured errors, Reflexion) hit the limit most often (51 and 54 of 60). C8 is the
only learning condition that stays close to the fixed harness (31 of 60), and it is also the only
one whose success is close to it.

\paragraph{Self-editing makes the prompt much longer.}
With C1, qwen uses three times as many prompt tokens per task as with the fixed harness (44{,}875
against 14{,}240). Part of this comes from the longer strategy and the extra memory, skills and
sub-agents, and part from the extra steps, since every step re-sends the prompt. Gemma's prompts
grow 2.5 times (3{,}403 to 8{,}400). In C8 the growth is smaller on both models.

\paragraph{The full system costs about twice the time on qwen.}
C8 takes 28.1 seconds per task against 14.5 for the fixed harness, plus about 20 minutes of learning
per seed (learning tasks, refiner calls and gate reruns). On gemma C8 is even slightly faster than C1, because most
edits were undone.

\paragraph{Format errors are rare, except after edits.}
Calls to a tool that does not exist were almost absent for qwen (1 at evaluation in all runs) and
for gemma with the fixed harness and C8 (0). Gemma with C1 made 14 such calls (see below).

\paragraph{The small model rarely uses what the refiner builds.}
In C1, qwen's own refiner wrote skills, but qwen called them only 3 times in total. Skills were used
more when they were written by the larger refiner (45 calls) or when memory was retrieved by
relevance (20 calls). With verified skills, the check itself worked: it rejected 8 broken skills,
for example code that used folder paths that do not exist. But qwen still called the kept skills only
3 times. With structured errors alone, qwen never called a skill or a sub-agent.

\paragraph{Gemma sometimes calls skills by the wrong name.}
Gemma with C1 made 14 calls to unknown tools at evaluation. In these calls it used the \emph{name of
a skill or sub-agent} (for example \texttt{file\_system\_organizer}) as if it were a tool, instead of
calling it through \texttt{run\_skill} or \texttt{call\_subagent}. The edited harness thus added a
new kind of error that the fixed harness never had.
